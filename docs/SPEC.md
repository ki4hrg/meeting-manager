# Meeting Manager: workflow and spec (greenfield successor to Meeter)

## Context

Meeter (`../Meeter`) does agenda building well but grew to ~82k lines, 49 tables, 110 MCP tools and a six-stop workflow per meeting. Its core problems are in the schema, not at the edges:

- Nothing can wait on a **person**; saving up questions for a sporadic meeting has no home.
- "Raise this next time" has four representations.
- Commitments, questions and decisions are three differently shaped tables, so one thing that changes type scatters its history, and each demands an owner and anchor up front.
- Todoist sync rewrites text, date and project on every run and reads back only completion (`lib/connect/todoist/sync.ts:449-473`).
- A Granola note only files itself on an exact calendar-event match, so drop-ins become manual "unfiled notes".
- Post-meeting processing is a ten-step Claude-driven skill.
- The UI is flat and undifferentiated.

Decisions made with John:

- **Greenfield, start empty.** Lift working code from Meeter; migrate no data.
- **Hybrid interface:** web UI plus Claude via MCP.
- **Granola is always the live screen.** The tool hands it a pasted private agenda and has no run view in the MVP.
- **Processing is automatic, then reviewed** on one screen.
- **Todoist:** two-way sync.
- **Keep** multi-day sessions, timed agendas, group ownership, project lookahead in agendas and recaps.
- **Post-MVP:** Google Doc publish and comment harvesting. **Dropped:** projector, multiple logins.
- John is the only user; everything outgoing is email or a Google Doc.

This version incorporates an independent review; its changes are marked *(review)*.

## Core model

### One item type, four kinds

A single `items` table. Only `context` and `text` are required.

| Kind | Meaning ("who has the ball?") | Closes when |
|---|---|---|
| `topic` | I want to raise, discuss or report this. Includes progress updates. | Discussed and finished |
| `question` | Something is unresolved and an answer is owed. "Needs a decision" is a question. | Answered (answer recorded) |
| `action` | A named party owes a deliverable. | Done or dropped |
| `decision` | Something was settled. A record, never "open". | Never; can be superseded |

- **Action vs question is decided by what closes it.** A question closes when information or a choice comes back. An action closes when a task is performed and there is nothing to report beyond "done". "These three need to meet to decide the lunch menu" is a question owned by the three. The extraction prompt applies this rule.
- **Topic, question and action convert in place**, keeping the record and history.
- **A decision is always its own record with its own wording** *(review)*. An answered question stays a question with its answer; "Record as decision" creates a linked decision pre-filled from the answer. This avoids a decision titled "What is the lunch menu?".
- **Spawning** links a child by `parent_item_id` (a decision spawns an action). The item page shows the whole thread.
- **Report back** *(review)*: when closing a question or recording a decision, an optional "report to" (person or group) spawns a child topic for that party carrying the resolution. This is how an answer from one meeting reaches another.
- **Fields:** `kind`, `status` (open, done, dropped; decisions: decided, superseded), `closed_at`, `superseded_by_item_id`, `text`, `detail`, `resolution`, `project_id`, `due_on` (always a deadline), `target_meeting_id` or `target_series_id`, `origin_meeting_id`, `parent_item_id`.
- **`item_parties`**: a person or a group, with role `owner` (owes the action/answer) or `with` (raise it with them; on an item John owns, the party it is owed to). Several allowed.
  - *(John, 2026-10-05)* **Shown by kind, stored the same.** The arrow points at the recipient: on an action or topic, its `with` (who it is for); on a question, its owners (who it is asked of); on a decision, its owners (who decides). Whoever leads stands first: an action's first owner, a question's asker (`with`), a decision's raiser. An action's or topic's further owners are collaborators and wear a two-way mark (⇄, issue #4). Forms and the item page label the roles by kind (`lib/items/roles.ts`), each saying what the person does: Assigned to / For on an action, Raised by / Raise with on a topic (collaborators on either: Together with, issue #47; plain "With" read too close to "Raise with"), Asked by / Answer needed from on a question, Raised by / Decided by on a decision. *(John, 2026-10-07)* The item page heads each group of chips with its role in words, so it reads on a phone; cards keep the arrow and name no role. A party's role is changed from its chip, recorded once as a role change. "Owner = whoever asks" was considered and not taken: "owed" (Today, hubs, the recap's follow-ups, Todoist) is built on the owner owing the answer.
  - *(John, 2026-10-06, issue #24)* **A question asked of no one in particular.** An open question with no owner, where John is the `with` party or there are no parties at all (an announcement still to come, say), counts as **Waiting on** on Today; the horizon rules decide when it shows (a due date brings it up, parked comes back on its day, undated sits in Later). Its card shows a no-one-in-particular mark (broadcast icon) where who it is asked of would be. Nobody owes it: the agenda never places or suggests it as owed and follow-ups never list it, though it can be aimed at a meeting by hand. No placeholder person or group, and no "source" field; the detail can say where the answer will come from. Once an answerer is added it is an ordinary Waiting on item.
  - *(John, 2026-10-19)* **Asking a question someone else owes.** One matter can be a question for the bishop, an action for John to put it to him, and later a decision; it stays a thread of linked records, never one record changing kind. **Ask** on an open question whose owner is not John makes John an action ("Ask Bishop Wright: …", owned by John, `with` whoever he asks: the owner by default, or an assistant or someone to copy), spawned from the question so it shows in its thread, and parks the question until that action is done (`later_until_item_id`). The ask syncs to Todoist like any action; the question, owed by someone else, never does. Asks are made one by one, since no two are alike (a calendar ask for an assistant, an email copying someone, a word in passing); two questions going in one email can share one ask (**join**). A question with no ask is simply his to answer, raised when he is there. Ticking the ask brings its questions back as Waiting on. At a meeting the person it is asked of attends, every question they owe is offered as theirs would be, asked or not, and the ask is not (`lib/agenda/candidates.ts`); marked discussed there from the meeting page, it counts as asked: it is Waiting on, and an ask made for it alone is done. Answering offers to close the ask it waited on (checked when nothing else waits on it), **Told them** (John has passed the answer on), and a **Decision** recorded from it in its own words. Processing's review offers Ask on an accepted question. Batching asks per person was considered and not taken.
  - *(John, 2026-10-19)* **Ongoing questions.** Some questions are worked through several conversations with several people ("What are the 2028 pilgrimage dates?") and are no one's to-do in the short term. Such a question is marked **ongoing** (`items.ongoing`, questions only, cleared by converting): it stays a question and closes with its answer like any other, but nobody owes it, so it is left out of I owe, Waiting on, follow-ups and Todoist (switching it on closes a task it had). It is raised like a topic: suggested at meetings carrying its project or people, placed when aimed. Its steps are the items that come out of it (Follow-up), often questions with asks of their own; what is learned along the way is a note on it, and processing proposes a note rather than an answer until a meeting settles it. It shows as a question mark going round (`KindIcon ongoing`), in an "Ongoing questions" section in the project hub's side column, and under Ongoing on `/items`. "Open question" was considered as the name and not taken: every unanswered question is open.
  - *(John, 2026-10-19, issue #47)* **Who owes what.** An action is owed by its owners, collaborators included; a question that is not ongoing by its owners. **On a topic, only the lead owner owes it, and only when that isn't John; collaborators never owe.** A topic someone else raises ("Adlene to raise it with extended cabinet") is hers to raise and to bring back what came of it, so it is in her "they owe", John's Waiting on, follow-ups and the recap's owed list, worded there as "raise with … and report back". A collaborator ("Together with") takes part and owes nothing: "Me, together with Adlene, raise with Faith" is owed by nobody. A topic John raises is his own to bring and owed by nobody: it is on the person's Raise, not in I owe. A decision is a record and owed by nobody. One rule, read everywhere (`isOwed`, `owersOf` in `lib/items/horizon.ts`): Today, the hubs, `/items`, follow-ups, the recap and the brief's who-owes-what tag; the roles stay as stored. An owed topic comes up at John's next meeting with its raiser, as owed items do (`lib/agenda/candidates.ts` matches any party, so a collaborator's meetings bring it up too), and closing it there (Done, or a Decision if one came of it) ends it: there is no separate follow-up topic. Todoist is unchanged: topics never sync. "A topic is never owed" was considered and not taken: a topic someone else is to raise elsewhere is owed back.
- **`item_events`** logs every step: created, discussed in meeting X, note added, converted, answered, completed, spawned. A topic discussed but unfinished stays open with a "discussed" event.
- Questions, actions and owed topics share one shape and appear together in a single "owed" list per person, group and project, each under who owes it (`owersOf`).
- Items with no party, project or target sit in the **Inbox**. The Inbox also holds the Granola notes no folder filed (see Drop-ins): they belong to no context until filed, so they show whichever context is selected. *(John, 2026-10-19, issue #51)* The Inbox lists its items and the meetings to review from every live context. Every item query takes a `contextId`. Nothing is listed across contexts, except on home (`lib/today.ts`) and the Inbox, which only read and mark each row's context (`ContextBadge`); each row opens in its own context. Quick-add there still files into the selected context, and filing a held note previews against it.

### People, groups, contexts

- **Contexts** are a hard boundary; projects, groups, meetings and items carry `context_id`.
- **People** are global, with `person_emails`; one person row is marked as John.
- **Groups** (`groups`, `group_members`) are context-scoped and act as parties alongside people. A meeting or series can belong to a group, which supplies its roster.
- **Capture context:** UI quick-add uses the selected context. MCP `capture_item` takes a context, or infers it from a named project or group.

### Agendas pull candidates; there are no anchors

*(review: bounded to stop flooding and leaks)*

**Placed automatically:** only items aimed at a meeting: this one, its series (the next occurrence), or a project's next meeting. *(2026-10-04)* Nothing is placed for its person, group or project alone, a 1:1 included: an item to raise with someone that isn't ripe yet stays a suggestion at each of their meetings until John places it. "Prep a meeting with…" is the exception, since asking for it is asking for the batch.

**Suggested** (collapsed list grouped by person, group and project; one click to place or to drop the item; *(2026-10-04)* there is no "not this time": one left alone is simply suggested again next time; *(John, 2026-10-19, issue #53)* under a person or a group, and under "more", items are grouped by where each stands between John and them (`stanceOf` in `lib/items/roles.ts`), each run under a muted mark and word: ← **To me** (they owe it to John: their action or a question asked of them, a topic they raise with him), → **From me** (his to bring: his action for them, a question they asked him, a topic he raises with them), ↩ **Reporting back** (they owe it to someone else), ⇄ **Together** (both on it, a collaborator, or someone else's to talk over). A run of several can be placed at once. It orders only; which items are candidates is unchanged):
- items whose project is one of the meeting's projects;
- items whose `with` or `owner` group is the meeting's group;
- items owned by an attendee, or `with` an attendee, that also carry one of the meeting's projects or groups;
- in a 1:1 (exactly two attendees, John and one other, and no group), anything owned by or `with` the other person. A meeting with a group is never a 1:1: what is for one member alone sits under "more" there, however few have joined so far.

**Not suggested:** person-only items in larger meetings (reachable under "more"), anything matched only through John himself, and *(2026-10-04)* anything aimed at another meeting, series or project: it is on its way there. An aim at a meeting that is over or gone is spent, and the item is matched as if it had none. *(2026-10-04)* Nor is anything that came out of this meeting: it is the meeting's outcome, and is offered at the next one.

**Leak rule:** "Follow-ups due" and the recap draw only from items placed on the agenda or originating in this meeting, never from the raw candidate pull.

State lives in `agenda_lines` (placed) and `meeting_dismissals`. Taking an item off an agenda never buries it: one aimed at that meeting alone loses its aim and is suggested wherever its people and project are; one aimed at a series or a project's next meeting leaves a dismissal, so it is placed on the next meeting instead (and can be restored to this one); anything else goes back to the suggestions. Dropping a suggestion shows a toast with Undo, which puts the item back as it was.

The bishop case: capture "ask @bishop about X" over several weeks. The bishop's person page shows the batch. "Prep a meeting with…" creates an undated stub and places all of it.

### Meetings are light and created lazily

- Google Calendar is a read-only feed. A meeting row exists only when John preps it, a note arrives for it, or he logs it by hand.
- `series` holds a roster, default projects, group, a standing agenda skeleton and generated-block defaults. A calendar recurring event maps to a series; a calendar maps to a context, or to a project, series or group in it.
- `parent_meeting_id` (one level) keeps multi-day sessions. *(2026-10-02)* A meeting with sessions keeps its own start and end, so it lists as the whole event; its agenda is the rollup of its sessions': its page shows each session's agenda in turn (no agenda or suggestions of its own), the brief and agenda copy are the sessions one after another, follow-ups and the recap draw on all its sessions, and an item aimed at it goes to its next session that is not over.
- *(2026-10-02)* A meeting's time is a start and an end, not a start and a length; the end may be days later.
- **Stub to calendar** *(review)*: an undated stub can be attached to a calendar event, and the tool suggests it when a new event's people match. Attendee matching uses the series roster as well as invitees, since in-person 1:1s often have none.
- **Drop-ins** *(review)*:
  - A Granola note with no calendar match creates a meeting itself. If people or context are unknown, the review screen asks for them first, then runs matching against their open items.
  - **Log a meeting:** pick people, paste or type notes, and run the same processing. Covers unrecorded conversations and is the fallback if Granola fails.
  - *(2026-10-02)* A note in no folder at all, outside mapped Granola folders, or in folders mapped to two contexts, is filed on the meeting already made for its calendar event if there is one, wherever it is; otherwise it is held in the **Inbox**, in no context and unprocessed, until John files it into a context (which then matches or makes its meeting there as a mapped folder would) or dismisses it. Any auto-created meeting can be discarded.
  - A folder maps to a context, or to one project, series or group in it; a note from a project's or group's folder puts that project or group on its meeting, and a meeting made for a note from a series' folder joins that series (unless its calendar event already maps to a series). A subfolder follows the nearest mapped folder above it. *(2026-10-04)* A calendar maps the same way: an event prepped from a calendar mapped to a project, series or group takes it, a series' calendar putting every event on it in the series. A mapping to a series follows the series when it is moved; a series, group or project something is mapped to cannot be deleted.

### Tables (about 30)

`contexts`, `people`, `person_emails`, `groups`, `group_members`, `projects`, `milestones`, `project_links`, `documents`, `series`, `series_people`, `meetings`, `meeting_people`, `meeting_projects`, `meeting_dismissals`, `agenda_lines`, `items`, `item_parties`, `item_events`, `item_todoist_links`, `notes`, `processing_runs`, `proposals`, `connections`, `bindings` (calendar/folder/Todoist project per context, or per project, series or group in it), `integration_events`, plus `users`, `sessions`, the OAuth tables and `mcp_write_receipts`.

## Workflow

One pipeline; every stage is optional, with no rail.

| Stage | Drop-in | Routine 1:1 / staff | Sporadic leadership | Project / committee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Capture | n/a | Items tagged with people/projects | Items tagged with the person | Items tagged with the project |
| Prep | None | Skeleton + placed items; skim suggestions | "Prep a meeting with…" places the batch | Full timed agenda |
| Run | Granola only | Private agenda pasted in | Private agenda pasted in | Timed private agenda pasted in |
| Process | Automatic, or Log a meeting | Automatic | Automatic | Automatic |
| Review | One screen | One screen | One screen | One screen |
| Export | Optional | Optional | Optional | Recap to attendees |

- **Capture:** global quick-add (one `@` for a person, group, project or series; kind prefix optional; everything optional) and MCP `capture_item`. *(2026-10-03)* An `@` resolves people and groups first, then projects, then series (an exact name of any kind beats a prefix), so `@sarah` stays Sarah while `@check-in` reaches "Adlene/John Check-In" and raises the item at that series' next meeting. `#project` and `>series` still say which outright, and the completion writes them when an `@` would reach something else of the same name. *(2026-10-06)* A token that matches nothing is offered as new (`@`: a person or a group; `#`: a project; `>`: a series), named from the token (`@Neil-Campbell` is "Neil Campbell", fixable before it is made) and made by name alone in the selected context, in the same transaction as the item: a refused line makes nothing, and a name already taken is refused. Typed in full and entered without picking, it is refused as unknown and the same offer is made in place.
- **Agenda:** two levels (section, line). A line is free text or a reference to a live item, with public text, a public description, a leader (any person, John included) and a private note. The description and leader go out in the private and the shareable export; the private note only in the private one.
- **Timing (optional):** minutes per line, computed start times, pinned starts, overflow flagged.
- **Generated blocks**, computed on render, defaults set on the series (on for project/committee meetings, off for 1:1s and drop-ins):
  - *Follow-ups due:* what attending people and groups owe, from placed items.
  - *Since last time:* decisions made, items closed and still open from the previous occurrence. *(John, 2026-10-19)* It reads the meeting's **circle**, its series, its groups and its projects (up and down the project tree, as the lookahead does), not only its series: "the previous occurrence" is the latest earlier meeting sharing any of them, and to what that meeting raised it adds what was settled since on anything the circle raised (raised at, placed on or aimed at its meetings, carrying the project or naming the group, or in the thread of such an item): questions answered and decisions recorded between the two meetings. An answer the circle already heard (given in one of its meetings) or one marked Told them is left out, and a decision stands in for the answer it was recorded from. This is how an answer reaches the planning team's next meeting, in place of defaulting Report to. A meeting of no series has it on when it has a group or a project.
  - *Lookahead:* upcoming milestones and deadlines for the meeting's projects, plus the next linked meetings.
- **Private and shareable:** *(John, 2026-10-05)* a meeting's page has one **Export**: presets, the parts it includes, and where it goes (clipboard as Markdown and rich text, PDF, email, a Markdown file). The parts are attendees, start times, descriptions and leaders, private notes, who owes what, follow-ups, since last time and lookahead. **Private** is everything: the agenda with planned start times, John's private notes and what attendees owe, for his own notepad. **Shareable** is what can be sent round: no private notes, nothing about who owes what, no follow-ups or since last time. A private part carries a lock. The last choice is remembered per kind of export. An item's kind is never written as a `Q:`/`A:` prefix, whatever is chosen.
- **Scratchpad:** Granola's own notepad, read through the API's `private_notes_markdown`. Processing sorts it three ways:
  - drafted content (a brainstormed event agenda) is saved whole as a `document` on the project hub, never split into items;
  - an unresolved circle-back becomes a proposed question or topic;
  - follow-ups to schedule become proposed actions for John or a "prep a meeting with…" stub.
- **Precise capture:**
  - *Verbatim lines:* `D:`, `Q:`, `A:`, `T:` lines in Granola's notepad are parsed **by code** before the model runs *(review)*, created as settled proposals with exact wording, and passed to the model as already captured. Their `detail` is the model's best-effort summary of the related discussion.
  - *Quick-add:* an item captured in the tool during a meeting attaches to it and is treated as settled. *(2026-10-04)* A meeting's page has **Outcomes** once it has started: everything that came out of it (accepted proposals, Log, and what is added there), with an Add that records the item's `origin_meeting_id` and does not aim it at the meeting or place it on the agenda. `capture_item` takes `from_meeting` for the same.
- **Processing** *(review)*:
  - Runs as a background job, never inside the webhook response. Input: private notes, summary, transcript, agenda, and open items for the attendees and projects. Model configurable.
  - Output: new items, updates to existing items, a summary per agenda line.
  - `processing_runs` is keyed by note id and content hash. Each proposal has a fingerprint and rejections are remembered, so a re-delivered or edited note proposes only new material.
  - A meeting can hold several Granola notes. A note for a multi-day event asks which session at review.
  - A reconcile job catches missed webhook deliveries.
- **Review:** one screen; accept, edit or reject each proposal, or approve all. Proposals whose owner was guessed are marked on the owner and excluded from approve-all. Accepted proposals can be undone. Nothing reaches real items or Todoist before approval.
- **Export:** recap computed on demand: summary, decisions, owed items grouped by person and group, lookahead. *(John, 2026-10-05)* Its prose (the summary and what was said under each line) can be reworded from the recap page; the edits are kept on the meeting (`recap_edits`), stand over later runs part by part, and can be cleared. Items are edited as items. It goes out through the same Export, with its own parts (attendees, summary, discussed, decisions, follow-ups, closed, lookahead): copy as rich text or Markdown, PDF, a Markdown file, or `mailto:`, which fills recipients and subject while the body goes to the clipboard. Also a per-person "your items" snippet.

## Todoist sync

- Syncs open actions and questions that name John as an owner. Group-only ownership does not sync.
- `due_on` maps to Todoist's **deadline** (`deadline_date`, verified in the API; needs Todoist Pro, which the account has). The tool sets the scheduled date once at creation (the day the item is approved, so it appears in Today for triage) and never reads or writes it again.
- Title, deadline and completion merge three ways against the snapshot in `item_todoist_links`; Todoist wins when both sides changed.
- *(review)* Remaining rules:
  - item done, dropped, converted to a decision, or John removed as owner: the task is closed;
  - a question ticked off in Todoist moves to "needs answer" in the tool, not closed;
  - reopen detection looks back 30 days; recurring tasks are unlinked; a deleted task unlinks quietly;
  - the Todoist project is set at creation from `bindings` and never moved; a sub-project with no binding of its own uses its nearest bound ancestor's, then the context's *(2026-10-04)*;
  - dates are computed in John's time zone, not UTC. *(2026-10-04)* The time zone is set on Settings (`users.time_zone`, prefilled from the browser; done 2026-10-04); the process `TZ` is only the fallback.
- *(John, 2026-10-18; built on `claude/todoist-sync`)* Settled while building it:
  - **Connection:** the instance's own registered Todoist app (OAuth, `TODOIST_CLIENT_ID`/`TODOIST_CLIENT_SECRET`), not a personal token, so Todoist's webhook can say when a task changes. Webhooks are only a nudge (Todoist says they can be late, out of order or lost): a delivery kicks the sync, which reads the change feed (`/api/v1/sync`, incremental by `sync_token`) as it always does. The sync also runs after every change made here, and every 10 minutes as the net. Tokens from a new app last an hour and the refresh token rotates, so a refresh holds a lock on the connection row. One Todoist account.
  - **Ownership:** John's own person row among the owners. A group as owner neither adds nor takes away: John and a group (his or not) syncs; a group alone, even his, does not.
  - **Existing items are not sent.** On connect, the items already open and syncing are set aside as *unmatched*. Settings → Match lists them; each is linked to a task John already has (the likeliest, by shared words, offered first; any open task can be searched), sent as a new task, or skipped. Linking takes the task's text and deadline (Todoist wins) and leaves it in its project. An item page, and `update_item`'s `todoist`, do the same for any syncing item with no task.
  - **Later:** a parked item's task is made with no scheduled date, so it does not land in Todoist's Today; its deadline follows `due_on` as always.
  - **Unbound:** an item whose project and context have no Todoist project goes to the Inbox.
  - **Removed:** an accept undone deletes the task it made, if still open; an item deleted after being closed leaves its closed task alone.
  - **What closes a task, Todoist cannot undo:** a task reopened in Todoist reopens a done, dropped or needs-answer action or question; one closed because John is no longer an owner, or because it became a topic, is closed again.
  - **Disconnect** removes every link and binding; the tasks stay in Todoist, and connecting again starts the matching over.
  - **Comments** *(issue #52)*: a linked task's comments (Todoist's notes) are read one way into `todoist_comments`, never `item_events`, and shown in the item's history among its own steps, marked as Todoist's. One deleted in Todoist goes here too; one on a task with no item is not kept. A task's existing comments are read whole on the pass after it is linked (Match, or any link from before comments were read: `comments_read_at`), a few tasks a pass; after that the change feed carries them. Writing comments back is not done.
